# ANVAX — Positioning Brief v2 (Global)

*The canonical source for how we talk about Anvax — internally, in outreach, in decks, on the website. Supersedes the May 2026 India-first brief. Last updated September 2026.*

## Why this version exists

Through mid-2026 Anvax was positioned as "the sovereign AI platform for India's regulated enterprises," with an NBFC wedge and RBI FREE-AI / DPDP as the compliance story. The India GTM was tried and proved hard to crack, so from September 2026 revenue focus moves to global markets: US, UK/EU, Middle East (UAE/KSA), and Southeast Asia/APAC. The vertical widens from BFSI-only to horizontal enterprise AI.

India does not disappear. It becomes one jurisdiction pack and one proof point ("built under the hardest data-residency regime in the world"), not the identity of the company.

## How to use this document

Same three rings as before. Ring 1 is what you lead with 90% of the time. Ring 2 expands when someone leans in. Ring 3 is for investors, senior hires, and partners. Architecture, deployment matrices, jurisdiction packs, and regulator material remain supporting material — later in the conversation, never first.

# Ring 1 — The One Sentence

Use this for: cold outreach openers, homepage hero, intro line at events, the answer to "so what do you do?"

| **Anvax is the self-hosted, governed AI workspace for enterprises that cannot put their data into ChatGPT, Glean, or Copilot** — search, chat, workflows and agents on your own documents, in your own cloud or data centre, with an audit trail your regulator will accept. |
| --- |

### What this sentence does

"Self-hosted" kills the "our data leaves the building" objection before it is raised, and it is the one thing the incumbents structurally cannot offer. "Governed" says there is a control and audit layer, not just a chatbot. "Enterprises that cannot put their data into…" defines the buyer by constraint rather than by geography or industry, which is what makes it work horizontally and globally. Naming ChatGPT, Glean, Copilot sets the competitive frame and disqualifies them in the same breath. "Your regulator" keeps the compliance depth without naming any single regulator, so the same sentence works in London, Dubai, Singapore, and New York.

# Ring 2 — The Three-Part Story

Use this for: discovery calls, deck slides 2–4, fundraise teasers, the answer to "tell me more." Three pillars, not five.

## Pillar 1 — The Wedge (what we sell today)

A governed AI workspace: search, chat, workflows, and agents running on the customer's own documents and systems, deployed inside their perimeter. The buyer is any enterprise where a CISO, GC, or compliance head has said "no" to SaaS AI: regulated financial services, healthcare, legal, public sector, defence-adjacent, and any mid-market company with a data-sensitivity policy.

It replaces the ungoverned "everyone pasting into ChatGPT" problem, which is now a board-level concern in every jurisdiction, not just India.

## Pillar 2 — The Governance Layer (what makes us defensible)

This is the part Glean and Copilot cannot bolt on, and the part that turns a workspace into infrastructure. Four moats, compounding:

- **Organisational memory** — the workspace gets smarter per customer; a competitor starting from zero cannot replicate it.
- **Audit and control plane** — immutable audit chain, per-tenant isolation, policy enforcement on prompts and retrieval, model-risk controls, PII redaction. Embedded in the customer's audit process; switching means re-certifying.
- **Jurisdiction packs** — control mappings and regulatory feeds per market: EU AI Act, GDPR, DORA (EU); FCA/PRA and ICO guidance (UK); NIST AI RMF, SOC 2, SR 11-7-style model risk (US); PDPL, SAMA, CBUAE (Gulf); MAS FEAT and PDPA (Singapore/APAC); RBI FREE-AI and DPDP (India). This replaces "India-stack depth" as the moat and is what lets one product sell into four regions.
- **Connector depth** — the global enterprise stack (Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Slack, Confluence, SharePoint, Salesforce, Snowflake, Jira) plus regional and vertical sources. Each one takes weeks to build correctly.

Vertical packs (BFSI, legal, healthcare) still exist but are now a layer on top of the horizontal platform, built from real customer feedback, not the headline.

## Pillar 3 — The Deployment Story (why we win the security-conscious buyer)

Three tiers, one product. Anvax owns no GPUs — we ship the platform, the customer or a partner ships the iron. That is how a Series-B SaaS company that wants a private VPC and a Gulf bank that demands air-gap can both buy the same product.

| **Tier** | **What it is** | **Who it's for** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Private cloud** | Dedicated deployment in the customer's own AWS / Azure / GCP account, any region | Mid-market enterprises, scale-ups with a data-sensitivity policy |
| **Sovereign cloud** | Regional sovereign / in-country GPU cloud partners (Gulf, EU, India, SEA) | Regulated financial services, public sector, data-residency mandates |
| **On-prem / air-gapped** | Customer data centre, no outbound network | Banks, defence-adjacent, critical infrastructure, government |

*Lead with the product. Mention the tiers only when sovereignty or residency objections come up — never as the opener.*

# Market Sequencing — recommendation, not yet decided

All four regions are in scope. Trying to open all four at once with a small team is the same conflation the May brief warned about (Wedge vs. Vision), just applied to geography. Recommended sequence, to be confirmed by Yogesh:

**Wave 1 — Middle East (UAE/KSA) and UK/EU.** Strongest regulatory pull for a sovereign/self-hosted story (PDPL, SAMA, EU AI Act, DORA, GDPR), active government-backed sovereign-cloud programmes, and in the Gulf a large India-connected network and overlapping working hours. Sales cycles are tolerable and the buyer already has "data cannot leave" written into policy.

**Wave 2 — US.** Biggest budgets, but Glean/Copilot/Writer own the mindshare and the buyer needs an explicit reason to self-host. Enter via the security-and-compliance buyer (CISO, GC) in financial services, healthcare, legal, and government contractors, once there are Wave 1 logos to point to.

**Wave 3 — Southeast Asia / APAC.** Singapore (MAS) as the beachhead; dynamics resemble India's regulated BFSI, so the existing playbook transfers. Lower price points, so best as an expansion market once the product is proven elsewhere.

# Five Conflations to Avoid

1. **Wedge vs. Vision.** "Self-hosted governed AI workspace" (today) and "operating layer for enterprise intelligence" (2035) are both true but are not the same sentence. Lead with the wedge.
2. **Product vs. Deployment.** Anvax is a product; the tiers are fulfilment. Customers buy Anvax and get routed to a tier.
3. **Compliance product vs. Compliance posture.** Anvax is not a compliance tool (Vanta, Drata, Sardine). It is an enterprise AI platform that is deeply governance-aware. Position against Glean and Copilot.
4. **Regulator pitch vs. Customer pitch.** Control mappings and regulator packs are stage-3 material for auditors and risk committees, not slide 2 of a discovery call.
5. **New: Horizontal vs. Vertical.** Going horizontal does not mean generic. The buyer is still defined by a constraint — "cannot use public AI" — and the proof is still vertical depth. Do not let "any enterprise" turn into "no particular enterprise" in the copy.
